NE NASI MON / 根無し者

Food knowledge travels with people.

It changes, adapts and takes root again.

About Nenasimon +

Nenashimono (根無し者) literally means someone without roots.

For me, being rootless does not mean being without connection. I am interested in the knowledge and practices people carry with them — ways of cooking, preserving and fermenting; tastes and memories; ways of looking at an ingredient.

When these practices encounter a new place, they meet different ingredients, climates, cultures and people. Something is carried forward, something changes, and something new can grow from the encounter.

Selected work: Greece, 2024 +

Halkidiki & Thessaloniki / Food residency

Learning a place through cooking.

In spring 2024, I spent several weeks in northern Greece, cooking with ingredients from the surrounding landscape and learning alongside local cooks.

I arrived with practices I already carried with me — Japanese home cooking, fermentation, sourdough and preserving — and began working with what I found there: local rice and sardines, semolina, herbs, flowers, mulberry leaves and coastal plants.

Rather than reproducing Japanese food in Greece, I used these practices as ways of looking, asking questions and experimenting.

More about my sake practice +

I am a certified Kikizakeshi (SSI sake sommelier), working with restaurants, hospitality professionals and consumers to make sake easier to understand, serve, pair and talk about.

My background as a cook is an important part of this work. I approach sake in relation to food and to the context in which people actually encounter and drink it.

Approach to teaching +

I am interested in tacit and embodied knowledge: what is learned through watching, touching, tasting, repeating and participating, rather than through written instruction alone.

Workshops are therefore spaces for making and learning together, rather than simply delivering recipes or information.

ONGOING PROJECT

Zestii Kitchens

Co-founder / Organiser

Building shared infrastructure for small food entrepreneurs in Finland.

About Zestii Kitchens +

I co-founded Zestii Kitchens as a social enterprise exploring how food knowledge becomes visible, how people can build livelihoods from the knowledge they already carry, and what kind of shared infrastructure makes that possible.

Through shared kitchen infrastructure, café operations, pop-ups, workshops and catering, Zestii creates ways for independent food practitioners to test, develop and offer their work.

ABOUT / THE PRACTICE

One practice, different forms.

Across cooking, sake, education and community organising, my work is concerned with how food knowledge is carried, shared and kept alive.

Much of this knowledge is tacit and embodied — learned through hands, repetition, observation, taste and participation.

My approach has been strongly shaped by Slow Food's emphasis on local food cultures, biodiversity, traditional knowledge and the people who sustain them.

I am active in the Slow Food movement in Finland and the Nordics.

Biography +

Minaë Tani-LaFleur is a Helsinki-based food practitioner and certified Kikizakeshi (SSI sake sommelier).

Her practice moves between cooking, fermentation, sake, education and community-based food projects. Rooted in Japanese home cooking and shaped by living and working across different places, her work explores how food knowledge travels, adapts and encounters local ingredients and practices.
